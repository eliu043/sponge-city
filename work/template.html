Street tree stormwater interception simulation

An attempt to visualize how street trees act as a network to mitigate stormwater runoff. This is using a simplified version of the NYC Parks Department's methodology, which uses the USDA Forest Service's i-Tree software.

It starts from the 2015 street tree census for upper Manhattan, estimates how much rain each tree's canopy catches in a year, and runs that forward to 2045 as trees grow, die, and are replanted.

The rule

For every living tree, every year, the page works out:

  • crown (m) from the tree's species equation, with trunk diameter converted to cm (1 in = 2.54 cm). Equations are from the Urban Tree Database, Northeast (Queens, 2005).
  • canopy (m²) = π × (crown ÷ 2)²
  • stormwater (gal/yr) = canopy × rain (in) × 0.0254 (in to m) × interception × 264.17 (m³ to gal)
  • growth: the tree's age from its trunk diameter, then the diameter at age + 1 minus the diameter at that age. Growth is never less than zero, and a tree stops growing once it reaches the largest trunk diameter its age equation was fitted to (without this, a few trees grow without limit).

Equation shapes: linear, quadratic and cubic are polynomials in dbh or age; "loglog" is exp(a + b × ln(ln(x + 1))); "expow1" is read here as exp(a + b × x). The last two are this sandbox's reading of the Urban Tree Database; the constant c in them is not used.

What the data and the rule leave out

This sandbox is based on a number of simplifications, including:

  • trees that stop growing at the edge of their equation's range, though real trees keep growing;
  • a flat figure for stormwater interception, the same for every species, season and storm (one Callery pear caught 15% and one cork oak 27% in the same study);
  • no awareness of local drainage, soils, pavement or topography that would affect runoff;
  • a simplified model of where trees die and where they are planted: deaths and plantings are random, planting only happens on 2015's dead-tree and stump sites, and a tree that dies during the run does not open up a new site;
  • no knowledge of the broader networks of policy and care that support the NYC street tree system;
  • 1,559 trees (of 12,093) of species outside the equations file use honeylocust equations, and 1,328 use a close relative's. Trees are only the street trees in the 2015 census, not park or private trees;
  • the census has 1 tree with a trunk over 60 in (a data entry error), which is left out; 258 trees fall outside every census block and appear on the tree map but not in the block totals.

Data and citations

  • 2015 Street Tree Census, NYC Parks / TreesCount!, cut to upper Manhattan: location, species, trunk diameter and status of each tree. NYC Open Data
  • 2010 Census Blocks, NYC Department of City Planning, cut to upper Manhattan: block outlines for the block view. NYC Planning
  • Tree growth and crown equations: Urban Tree Database, Northeast region (Queens, 2005), McPherson et al., USDA Forest Service. Research Data Archive
  • Method: NYC Parks, Street tree benefits; i-Tree (41 in of rain, observed at JFK in 2000; tree death rates of 2.8% a year under age 5 and 0.57% after).
  • Interception: Xiao, Q., McPherson, E.G., Simpson, J.R., Ustin, S.L. (2000), Winter rainfall interception by two mature open-grown trees in Davis, California, Hydrological Processes 14.
