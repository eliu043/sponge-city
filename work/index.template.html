The City As Sponge

A simplified cellular-automata simulation of rain falling on New York City. It shows how street-level elevation and impervious surfaces decide where stormwater collects, how it runs from one 500 ft cell to the next, and how it drains once the rain stops.

The record

Each of the — land cells is a 500 × 500 ft square of the city with fixed attributes:

  • Elevation (ft) — the average of a triangulated surface (TIN) built through — spot elevations from the NYC Planimetric Database (SUB_CODE 300000), sampled every 50 ft in the cell. data
  • Impervious fraction (0–1) — share of the cell covered by impervious land cover in the NYC impervious-surface geodatabases, measured on a 10 ft raster. data
    That data covers tax lots only. Area in a land cell with no lot record (about 25% of the city's land) is assumed to be street and sidewalk and counted as fully impervious. assumption
    Semi-pervious classes are weighted by rational-method runoff coefficients: gravel 0.45 (Iowa DOT Design Manual 4A-5), bare soil 0.25 (loose soil, rational-method tables), synthetic turf 0.95 (Ohio EPA draft guidance 8.3). assumption
  • Elevation confidence — high: ≥3 spot elevations in the cell and one within 150 ft of its center; medium: at least one in the cell; low: none, or outside the TIN. Low-confidence cells stay in the run.

Cells that are mostly water, or outside the city's land-cover record, are drawn in grey-blue. This is the only base map; there are no tiles.

The rule, every minute

  1. Rain. effective rain = rainfall ÷ 60 × runoff factor, where runoff factor = p + (1 − p) × impervious fraction and p is the pervious runoff fraction.
  2. Drainage. drained = min(depth, drainage ÷ 60), the same everywhere.
  3. Compare. water level (ft) = elevation + depth (in) ÷ 12. Only neighbors with a lower water level can receive water, so a low cell fills until it spills.
  4. Send. Water above the surface storage can move: outgoing = (depth − storage) × routing fraction, split among lower neighbors by their share of the total head difference.
  5. Update together. All transfers are collected first, then every cell updates at once. Depth never goes below zero.

At the shore and the city line, the missing neighbors are treated as ground at the cell's own elevation with no water on it, so water above the storage can leave. It is counted as boundary outflow. The balance rain added = stored + drained + boundary outflow is checked every step.

What it can't see

  • Real street surfaces. Streets and sidewalks aren't in the land-cover data; they are assumed fully paved, and at the shore some of that “street” may really be water or pier.
  • Buildings. Spot elevations are ground points, so water flows straight through building footprints. Roofs count as impervious, but their water lands on the cell's ground.
  • Sewers. Drainage is one uniform rate, not catch basins, pipe sizes or combined-sewer overflows. Sewers don't fill up or back up.
  • Soil and saturation. Pervious ground absorbs the same share of rain from the first minute to the last.
  • Detail below 500 ft. Curbs, basements, underpasses and individual low spots are averaged away.
  • Tides and storm surge. The rivers and harbor take any amount of water and never rise.
  • Speed of flow. Water moves by a fixed routing fraction each minute, not by slope and roughness (Manning's equation), so travel times are only relative.

Data: NYC Planimetric Database, Elevation Points (export 2026-10-07); NYC impervious-surface geodatabases for the five boroughs. Rule after the weighted cellular-automata approach in Gibson et al., “Accuracy and computational efficiency of 2D urban surface flood modelling based on cellular automata,” Procedia Engineering 154 (2016).
